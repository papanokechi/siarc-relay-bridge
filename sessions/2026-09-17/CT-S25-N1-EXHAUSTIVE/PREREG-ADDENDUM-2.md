## 1. What happened, stated plainly

**V3b failed too, and its inference is ill-posed for the same reason V3's was.**

V3b dropped each coefficient equation in turn and required the degree to move
for at least one. It moved for none. I pre-declared that this establishes cause
**(I), insensitivity**, and that every degree in this session is therefore
**VOID**.

On inspection that inference does not hold, and it fails in exactly the way V3's
did. The system is **six generators in four unknowns cutting out two points** —
overdetermined by two. On such a system, *no single generator need be load
bearing*. "The degree did not move when I removed one of six redundant
constraints" is fully consistent with a perfectly sensitive instrument.

**This is the second arm in this session to fail in the same class**, after
being authored with the first one explicitly in mind. That is the session's most
transferable finding, and it is recorded as such in `REPORT.md` §3 rather than
buried.

## 2. The rule I am NOT allowed to break, and how this respects it

I may not keep inventing arms until one gives the answer I want. So this
addendum does **not** add a third sensitivity arm. It **abandons the sensitivity
approach entirely** and replaces it with a different kind of test.

**Diagnosis of why sensitivity arms keep failing here.** A sensitivity arm asks
whether the observable moves when the *target system* is perturbed. On an
overdetermined system, perturbations that ought to be informative are absorbed
by redundancy, so a null response is uninterpretable no matter how the arm is
phrased. **Sensitivity is the wrong instrument test for this observable.**

**The right instrument test is a known-answer control** — the pattern already
established in this corpus at `CT-S22-STOKES` Stage 1, where the solver had to
reproduce the Kummer equation's Stokes multipliers against their closed forms
before it was used on anything. A known-answer control does not depend on the
target system at all, so overdetermination cannot absorb it.

If A–G pass, the counter is correct. Combined with:

- **V1**, which exhibits two **distinct** points that satisfy every generator
  **exactly** and lie off `q(q−1) = 0`; and
- the **eliminant** `27θ² + 108θ + 107`, matching the prediction registered in

algebraic closure, counted with multiplicity, by 2. Two distinct points are

produced the six generators — that is `CT-S16`'s, and is unchanged here. N1 asks
only whether `sp.solve`'s two solutions were all of them.

1     Introduction
Let b be a polynomial of degree d ≥ 1 with b(k) ̸= 0 for every integer k ≥ 1, and let Qn be the
denominators of the associated polynomial continued fraction,

This paper is about one such recurrence, the quadratic case b(n) = 3n2 + n + 1, and about the
differential equation its generating function satisfies. The results are of two kinds, and they are
established to two different standards. Saying which is which is the purpose of this introduction.

The differential equation, and the leaf. For the quadratic case the generating function satis-
fies a second-order linear ODE. After an explicit scalar reduction, the connection attached to that
ODE is shown to be the differential module of a specific member of van der Put–Saito’s rank-2

identification is of the full operator and not merely of its projective class; removing the projective
ambiguity is a separate step, carried out in §7. Painlevé V is the isomonodromy foliation of that
moduli space, and the deformation vector field is regular at the point, so exactly one leaf passes
through it. Transporting along that leaf and recomputing the trace coordinates x1 , x2 , x3 of the
monodromy and Stokes data leaves them invariant to better than 90 decimal digits, at two step
sizes and on both branches of a sign pair exchanged by an explicit Galois automorphism.

The two limits. Both are stated here rather than in a closing section, because a reader is entitled
to know them before reading the argument.

    1. Leaf membership is numerical. The invariance of the trace coordinates under transport
       is a high-precision numerical agreement, checked against a pre-registered falsifier and accom-
       panied by controls that displace the point off the leaf and that substitute a deliberately wrong
       deformation field. It is evidence, and it is strong evidence, but it is not a proof, and nothing
       in this paper upgrades it to one. The identification of the operator, by contrast, is exact and
       is proved by elimination.

    2. No Painlevé label is asserted for this recurrence. The classification question — which
       row of van der Put–Saito’s Table 2.1 the operator occupies — is addressed only negatively.
       §4 shows that one row is excluded for every admissible coefficient vector, by a Katz-invariant
       argument at z = 0. A matching multiset is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one, so
       excluding a row is not the same as assigning one, and no row is assigned.

2.2     Standing hypotheses
Three hypotheses are used, and they are stated separately because they are consumed separately.
Only (A1) is in force by default; (A2) and (A3) are invoked by name wherever they are needed.

(A1) b(k) ̸= 0 for every integer k ≥ 1.

(A2) No root νi is a positive integer; equivalently, Γ(1 − νi ) is finite for every i. (The Gamma
     function has poles at 0, −1, −2, . . . , so Γ(1 − νi ) is singular exactly when νi ∈ {1, 2, 3, . . . }.)
     Note (A2) ⇒ (A1).

(A3) ψ∞ = limn→∞ Qn /Bn exists and is nonzero.

the condition under which the constant K of §2.4 is defined at all. A statement that uses K and
cites only (A1) has omitted a hypothesis.

Γ denotes Euler’s Gamma function, the meromorphic continuation of Γ(s) = 0 ts−1 e−t dt (ℜs >
0), with simple poles at s = 0, −1, −2, . . . and Γ(m + 1) = m! for integers m ≥ 0. We write (dn)!

    For functions of n → ∞: f = O(g) means |f | ≤ C|g| for some constant C and all large n;

    A formal solution is a solution taken as a formal power (or power-and-exponential) series, with
no convergence asserted. The Newton polygon of a linear differential operator at a point is the lower
convex hull, in the plane of (order of differentiation, order of vanishing) of the coefficients, of the
points attached to its terms; its slopes govern the exponential growth of formal solutions at that
point. A germ coefficient is a coefficient of such a local expansion, i.e. of the germ of the solution at
the point in question, as opposed to a global invariant. The amplitude of an exponentially small or
large term is the prefactor multiplying its exponential, and c± denote the amplitudes attached to
the two branches of a square-root (rank 12 ) irregular singularity. A Stokes constant is the coefficient
with which one formal solution enters the Borel-plane singularity structure of another across a
Stokes line; it is a connection datum, not a local one.

2.8   Monodromy and trace coordinates
On the monodromy side, B1 , B2 , B3 are the monodromy matrices of the local system, one for each
of the three punctures, in van der Put–Saito’s notation at p. 2637. They carry ai = tr(Bi ), and the
affine cubic surface of monodromy data is coordinatised by

                      x1 = tr(B2 B3 ),     x2 = tr(B1 B3 ),     x3 = tr(B1 B2 ).                   (4)

These are the trace coordinates, and (4) says what they are traces of. Where the paper speaks of
comparison quantities it means x1 , x2 , x3 and nothing else.
    The letter B is thus used for the monodromy matrices Bi and, in (2), for the product Bn of
values of b. The two never occur in the same argument; the monodromy matrices always carry an
index in {1, 2, 3} and the product always an index n.

6.5   Membership, and the word it leaves standing

7     Removing the projective ambiguity

8     Monodromy, Stokes data, and the character variety
Sections 6 and 7 identified System C with a member of the van der Put–Saito family at a located
point of the chart U0 , and removed the projective qualifier from that identification. What has
not yet been produced is the monodromy data: the point of the character variety that System C
represents. This section produces it. It does so with a solver that is first validated against a case
with closed-form answers, and under a set of orientation and ordering conventions that are selected
by test rather than assumed.
    The distinction matters more here than anywhere else in the paper. Trace coordinates are
not convention-independent: a different choice of loop orientation, of the order in which local
monodromies are composed, or of which loop at infinity is called γ∞ , produces a different triple
for the same differential system. A stated triple without a stated convention is not a result. The
convention used below is therefore the output of §8.3, not an input to it.

it is the signature of a set that omits the truth rather than of a threshold set too tight.

9.6    An involution, and a structural route that does not close
If the branches give the same point up to an interchange of x1 and x2 , the natural question is

Remark 9.10 (The involution; S22-23, PROVEN). For the System C family at both branches
the denominator D(z) = z 2 − z is exactly invariant under φ(z) = 1 − z. Hence φ fixes the singular

     The candidate is natural, and it fails.

Remark 9.11 (No constant-gauge equivalence; S22-24, PROVEN). There is no analytic equiv-
alence between the two branches of the form A2 (z) = −G A1 (φ(z)) G−1 with G a constant gauge,
for any of the four candidate maps φ: the identity, z 7→ 1 − z, coefficient conjugation, and coeffi-
cient conjugation composed with z 7→ 1 − z. Since A is traceless, its determinant is the only gauge
invariant, and the necessary scalar condition on − det A fails for all four, with maximal coefficient
3.83, 0.416, 0.416 and 3.83 respectively.

Remark 9.12 (The negative is informative). A failed symbolic identity can mean “no such relation”
or “the simplifier did not get there”. The positive control separates them: σ applied to branch 1’s
invariant, minus branch 2’s, returns exactly zero through the same radicals and the same simplifier.
The machinery is therefore capable of returning zero on this class of expression when zero is the
answer, and the four nonzero results are findings rather than failures to simplify.

been refuted is the simplest form of the argument: a z-dependent gauge is untested and remains
open, and is listed as such in §11.

Recording the failure at the same weight as the success is the point: a reader who sees only the
successes cannot tell which of the paper’s identifications are supported by structure and which are
supported by digits.


10      Scope and limits
The preceding sections established a chain: a growth law for the denominators, a local classification,
a moduli space, a located point, the removal of a projective ambiguity, a point of the character
variety, and transport along the flow. This section states what that chain does and does not
establish. It is written so that a reader who stops here is not left to infer the strength of any link
from the confidence of the prose.

11.2     Independence of the monodromy convention

B     Computational methods
This appendix records the algorithms behind the numerical statements of §3, §8 and §9, the controls
that were run against them, and the residuals they achieved. It also discharges the forward reference
made in §8, where two algebraic identities were deferred to here.
    A statement of scope first, because it is easy to leave implicit and it should not be. What
follows states what was computed, at what precision, with which controls, and to what residual.
It does not make the computations reproducible from this text alone: reproduction requires the
scripts, which are recorded with the claims and not reprinted here. That is the ordinary condition
of a paper of this kind, and it is said plainly rather than left to be discovered.

B.1    The solver, and its known-answer test
Every monodromy and Stokes computation in §8 and §9 is produced by one solver. Before it was
applied to System C, it was run against a case whose answers are known in closed form.

Remark B.1 (Known-answer battery; S22-01, VERIFIED). The solver reproduces Kummer’s
monodromy, formal and Stokes data against closed forms: 32 of 32 checks pass at at least 35 digits,
with 44 to 151 digits observed, at both pre-registered parameter values.

    Two features of that test are worth naming. The parameter values were fixed before the run,
so the battery could not be tuned to the solver; and the quantities checked include Stokes data,
not only monodromy, so the irregular part of the machinery is exercised rather than assumed.

B.2    Truncation of the formal series
The formal solution at the irregular point is divergent, and it is used truncated. Where to truncate
is decided by the series itself.

Remark B.2 (Optimal truncation; S22-09, VERIFIED). The truncated formal series’ residual
sits at the minimal term of the series — ratio 1.29 against a bound of N 3 — and decays at the
predicted Gevrey rate e−|t|R between R = 160 and R = 140, with observed over predicted 1.259.




                                                 37
   The truncation radius is pre-registered rather than chosen after the fact, and this has a con-
sequence that surfaces in the transport computation: the truncation level it fixes is a floor on the
achievable agreement.

Remark B.3 (The floor is truncation, not precision; S22-17, VERIFIED). Raising the working
precision from 200 to 320 digits leaves the transport agreement and its ratio unchanged to every
printed figure — 90.8118, 91.8301, ratio 10.429999 — while every precision-limited control improves
by about 120 digits: one residual from 1.20 × 10−105 to 5.57 × 10−225 , a second from 3.96 × 10−112 to
7.13×10−232 , a third from 2.35×10−113 to 3.30×10−233 , while the α residual stays at 1.834×10−91 .
The floor is the optimal-truncation level 10−88.7 set by the pre-registered R, which no precision can
reduce.

     This is the paper’s clearest instance of a control that separates two explanations of the same
number. Had the residual been round-off, it would have fallen with the precision; the controls did
fall and it did not.

B.3    Two algebraic checks, and what went wrong with them
§8 deferred two identities to this appendix. Both are recorded here because each was, as first
written, defective — one vacuously true, one comparing incompatible factorisation orders — and
because the repaired versions do not carry the evidential weight the originals appeared to.

Remark B.4 (A check that could not fail; S22-04, PROVEN). Check A6a as first written was
algebraically vacuous: (1 + f1 f2 )/α = M22 equals (1 − det M )/m11 and so vanishes identically for
any unimodular M with M11 ̸= 0. It could not fail. This was proved by simplification to zero
together with an independent numeric arm.

Remark B.5 (A check comparing two orderings; S22-05, PROVEN). Check A7 as first written
used the source’s diagonal–lower–upper ordering, with trace α + (1 + f1 f2 )/α, against a computed
diagonal–upper–lower factorisation, with trace α(1 + f1 f2 ) + 1/α; the two differ by (α − 1/α)f1 f2 .
At the known-answer values the wrong ordering misses 2 cos(πb) by 0.9612, while the corrected one
holds to 1.15 × 10−29 , reproducing the observed failure at order one. The corrected identity is a
dependent restatement of three checks already made, not independent evidence.

    The last sentence is the reason both appear here rather than among the supporting evidence of
§8. A repaired check that restates what other checks already established is a consistency test on
the algebra, and counting it as a further confirmation would inflate the evidence.

B.4    Extrapolation and high-precision limits
The growth-law statements of §3 rest on extrapolating a ratio sequence, with the extrapolation
itself controlled.

representatives the growth law Qn /Γ(4n + 1) ∼ K(β4 /44 )n ns holds with s = β3 /β4 + 3/2. The
maximum of |smeasured − spredicted | over the eight is 7.118 × 10−16 , after 4 levels of ratio-2 Richard-
son extrapolation on the ladder n = 1250, 2500, 5000, 10000, 20000 at 80 digits. The pre-registered
falsifier |diff| > 10−6 did not fire.

Remark B.7 (The limit, to high precision; S3B-09, VERIFIED). The limit of the normalised
denominator sequence for the quadratic representative was computed to 2317.8 confirmed digits, by

    Two points of method are visible in the second of these. Confirmation is by agreement between

B.5    What the methods do not establish
Three limitations follow from the above and are stated here rather than left implicit.

  1. Agreement between two runs at different truncation is evidence of internal consistency at a
     stated precision. It is not a proof, and every label attached to such a statement in this paper
     is VERIFIED for that reason.

  2. The transport residual is bounded below by a pre-registered truncation radius, so the digits
     quoted in §10.3 are the instrument’s ceiling at that step size, not an approach to an exact
     statement.

  3. Every displayed formula in this paper is routed to exactly one form of evidence, and the routes
     are not equally strong. Of the 56 displays of §§0–10, 21 were re-derived symbolically from the
     script that computed them, 9 were verified against the printed page they were transcribed
     from, 11 are definitional, and 15 trace to a recorded claim only. Three of that last group
     — the exact q and p of §6.3 — were later re-derived coefficient by coefficient, leaving twelve
     short displays that are traced but not symbolically re-derived. Re-derived is used here in the
     strict sense: recomputed from the script and compared symbolically. A display that is traced
     has a claim asserting it; it does not have a recomputation.
